Task — Implement and Evaluate a Baseline-Preserving AMG Query Compression

We have completed the previous controlled experiment (prompts/new3.txt).

Its result was DO NOT PROMOTE.

The previous candidate improved fact coverage and Anemia retrieval, but it:

newly omitted the black-stool finding;
worsened GI Bleeding retrieval;
lost a previously included palpitations finding on another case;
reduced accepted evidence on another case;
introduced structural alias contamination such as sex → Sexual Health;
therefore must remain experimental.
Goal

Run ONE new, narrowly controlled experiment:

Determine whether we can compress the existing production AMG query without losing any facts that the current production query already embeds, while using the freed token budget to include previously omitted patient facts.

Call this baseline-preserving compression.

Do NOT attempt to solve every AMG problem in this task.

1. Production must remain unchanged

Do NOT modify the production retrieval path.

Do NOT change:

Do not promote the candidate automatically.

If the experiment fails, leave production exactly as it was.

2. Core rule: preserve the baseline embedded information

Start from the exact production query generated for each patient.

Determine the complete set of patient facts that are actually represented in that baseline query.

The new representation MUST NOT omit any baseline-included clinical fact merely because of role balancing.

For the saved case specifically, the candidate MUST preserve:

pallor;
all pain-related facts;
dizziness;
near-fainting;
fatigue;
activity limitation;
non-restful sleep;
black stools;
poor diet;
prior anemia.

The important requirement is:

No baseline-included clinical fact may be sacrificed merely to increase the number of total facts.

This is the main difference from new3.

3. Compression strategy

Use only deterministic text compression.

Allowed:

removing repetitive interrogative scaffolding;
converting structured question/answer syntax into compact literal statements;
removing redundant repeated wording;
grouping answers belonging to the EXACT SAME original question;
compact deterministic formatting.

Example:

Do you feel slightly dizzy or lightheaded? = Yes

may become:

dizzy/lightheaded = Yes

But do not infer:

anemia

or:

possible GI bleeding

or any diagnosis.

Preserve qualifiers such as:

recently;
ever;
family history;
timing;
negation;
unknown;
answer values.

Do not paraphrase medical meaning using a clinical dictionary or LLM.

4. Use remaining budget for omitted facts

After preserving every baseline-included fact, calculate remaining token capacity.

Only then add previously omitted facts.

Priority must NOT be based on diagnosis or medical assumptions.

Use deterministic order such as:

previously omitted patient history/antecedent facts;
previously omitted medication facts;
previously omitted contextual facts.

But if adding an omitted fact would cause ANY baseline-included clinical fact to disappear, do not add it.

The baseline information has priority.

5. Do NOT put structural metadata into the medical query

Do NOT emit literal structural headings such as:

Presenting:
Symptoms:
History:
Medications:
Context:

unless the experiment proves that they cannot interact with AMG aliases.

For this experiment, the safest default is:

Do not add role/header words to the embedded text.

Keep role information in the internal audit structure only.

This is specifically because new3 demonstrated:

sex → Sexual Health

and potentially:

Medications → medical alias

We do not want structural metadata influencing the medical embedding.

6. Alias handling

Do NOT broaden the previous all fix.

Do NOT rewrite the vendor alias system.

Do NOT add medical aliases.

Do NOT create topic-specific aliases.

For this experiment:

retain the existing experimental ordinary-all guard only if it is already isolated from production;
do not introduce additional alias behavior;
record any alias expansions produced by the candidate;
especially report structural/context contamination such as sex → Sexual Health.

The purpose of this task is query compression, not alias-system redesign.

9. Success criteria

Do NOT define success as simply "more facts."

A candidate is potentially better only if:

Required
Zero baseline clinical facts lost across the evaluated cases.
No production files are changed.
No diagnosis labels or benchmark labels are used.
No clinical inference is introduced.
No new unsafe alias behavior is introduced.
Existing AMG top-k and gate remain unchanged.
Strong positive signal

If the candidate also:

reduces token usage;
includes previously omitted facts;
improves Anemia retrieval;
improves Blood Thinners/Kidney Failure retrieval;
improves or preserves GI Bleeding retrieval;
preserves or improves accepted evidence across validation cases.
Failure

If the candidate loses any baseline clinical fact, especially a symptom/finding such as:

black stools;
palpitations;
chest pain;
vomiting blood;
respiratory symptoms;

record the regression and do NOT promote.

Do not compensate by changing top-k or threshold.
